What the margin can do 🧵

Five things worth knowing before your next long answer.

Threads, not sticky notes 💬

Highlight any part of an answer and ask about just that part — the reply streams into a box beside the page while your main chat stays untouched. And a thread is a real conversation: ask follow-ups, minimize it to a chip, resolve it when it's settled, reopen it next week. Everything is saved per conversation and finds its exact sentence again after a reload.

Ask one question across all of them 🧠

This is where your annotations quietly become a knowledge base. Check a few threads and labeled answers, type a prompt — "what patterns keep coming up?" — and Marginalia bundles them up, asks the AI you're on, and streams the answer right in the panel. Ask follow-ups; earlier answers stay on screen. Then download it as Markdown with a note of which items went in, or copy it and open a fresh chat.

Built for a hundred threads 🧘

Annotate as much as you like. Marginalia does work only for what actually changed on the page — so heavily-annotated conversations load fast, scroll without a stutter, and stay smooth to the last word of a long streamed answer. The same extension, made boring-fast.

Take everything with you 📦

Every conversation you annotate is quietly kept as a transcript on your computer — filling in as you scroll, even on sites that only show part of a long chat at a time. One click exports the conversation plus your comments as clean Markdown, ready for NotebookLM or Obsidian. And from Settings you can back up everything — threads and transcripts — to a single file, and bring it back on any machine.

Sound familiar? ✋

Six little frustrations we kept hitting ourselves — each one already fixed in a release you have.

"The answer arrives faster than I can read — and drags the page down with it."
fixed in 0.3.2
Turn on Calm scrolling: the view follows a couple of lines, then holds still. A round arrow marks the text piling up below — click it, or scroll yourself, to catch up.
"I typed half a question, opened the full-screen view — gone."
fixed in 0.3.1
Drafts follow you now. Margin box to full-screen and back — your half-typed message is right there, ready to finish.
"Enter sent my message in the middle of a code block."
fixed in 0.3.1
Inside an unclosed ``` fence, Enter makes a new line instead of sending. Ctrl/Cmd + Enter always sends.
"I know I asked this before… but in which chat?"
fixed in 0.3.0
The Across-chats tab searches every conversation you've annotated — by any word in a highlight, a question, or an answer.
"The input box swallowed the first line of my question."
fixed in 0.4.0
The composer keeps growing as you type — up to 40% of the window — and the comment box rises to make room, so everything stays visible.
"I scrolled up to reread something and the stream yanked me back down."
fixed in 0.3.2
The full-screen view and the panel respect your scroll position while an answer streams. Reading beats following.

And the rest of it 🧰

Small things you'll notice on day two.

Math that reads like math
\frac{a}{b} shows up as real fractions, Greek letters, and superscripts — in margin boxes and full-screen alike.
Markdown when you mean it
A per-message MD toggle decides whether what you send renders formatted or plain. It lights up when it's on.
Resize anything
Drag the threads panel by any edge or corner, and the full-screen view by its edges — your size sticks for the visit.
Minimize, resolve, reopen
Boxes shrink to chips, and opening one thread neatly tucks the others away.
Search inside a chat
Find any thread by any word in a highlight, a question, or an answer.
Ctrl+Z, even after sending
Undo in every composer brings back text you deleted or already sent.
Labels, editable everywhere
Chips with a ×, a pencil on threads, or /label right in the message field.
Dressed for each site
A flat look that borrows ChatGPT's, Claude's, and Gemini's own colors.
Your session or your keys
Logged-in Claude and Gemini need zero setup; add API keys in Settings when you want them (ChatGPT uses one).
Backup & restore
Export threads and transcripts to a single file; restoring merges safely and never touches your keys.
No server, no tracking
Everything lives in your browser; questions go only to your AI.
Firefox & Chrome
The same extension, both browsers — grab it from the Chrome Web Store or Firefox Add-ons.
